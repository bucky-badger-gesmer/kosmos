# Returning Candidate — You Interviewed Last Year and Declined

This is the one topic none of the other files cover, and it is the one thing every interviewer already knows about you. Assume all five have read last year's notes and that at least one will ask. Prepare it as carefully as any tech question. Done well, it is an *advantage*: you are a known quantity who came back on purpose.

---

## The three questions you will get

1. **"What changed?" / "Why are you back?"**
2. **"Why did you decline last time?"**
3. **"What would make you say yes this time?"** (sometimes phrased as "what would make you decline again?")

Alex (4:00) is the most likely to ask directly. Dan or Nick may ask a softer version. Patrick and Ben probably won't, but might mention remembering you, so have a warm one-liner ready.

---

## The answer shape (say it in under 60 seconds)

Three sentences, in this order. Write yours in the blanks and say it out loud until it sounds like you, not a script.

**1. What happened then, one factual sentence, no blame.**
> "Last year I got to the offer stage and turned it down because ______________________. That was the right call for me at the time."

*Fill in the real reason. Timing, compensation, a competing offer, a commitment you couldn't leave, the role scope. State it plainly. Do not badmouth the other option or hedge.*

**2. What has changed since, with specifics.**
> "Since then I've ______________________, and I've spent the last year going deeper on ______________________."

*Concrete material you can point to from this workspace:*
- Backend and AWS depth: studying for the AWS Solutions Architect Associate, plus hands-on labs on VPC networking, RDS Multi-AZ and read replicas, S3-triggered Lambda thumbnails, CloudFormation, and an autoscaling lab. This is the exact "backend development (e.g. AWS Lambda)" bullet in the JD.
- Full-stack side work: an Express + TypeScript + SQLite contacts API, built with strict TypeScript and a documented stack decision.
- AI tooling: an agent project (the `wes` work referenced in the Blank Metal prep) with MCP servers, sub-agents, and evals. GovDocs told you last year that AI tooling is encouraged. This is a natural bridge.
- Whatever grew in your day job at Augeo: a shipped feature, a production incident you owned, a review or testing practice you introduced.

**3. Why GovDocs specifically, now.**
> "I came back because ______________________."

*Options that are true and land well:*
- The compliance SaaS line is still early and you want to shape a product, not maintain one.
- The problem (jurisdictional variance × change over time) is genuinely interesting and you kept thinking about it.
- The team and the mentoring structure stood out last year.
- Hybrid in the Twin Cities with a company that has grown 18% a year and was named a Best Place to Work.

Pick two. The reason has to be consistent with why you declined. If you declined over comp, "the mission" alone will not be believed. Say something like "I understood the range better this time and the growth since then closes the gap."

---

## "What would make you say yes?"

Answer this directly. You intend to accept, so do not be coy. Something like:

> "Honestly, I'm here to get an offer and accept it. The things I'd want to confirm are ______________________ and ______________________, and I'd like to talk through the range."

Name one or two real things: the team you'd land on, the mentor pairing, the split between the Posting Service and the new SaaS. Naming nothing reads as either desperate or hiding something.

---

## Compensation

The posting says $100,000 to $120,000. Decide before Wednesday:

- **Your number.** One figure you would sign for. Write it here: ______________
- **Your floor.** The number below which you would decline again. Write it here: ______________
- **Consistency check.** If you gave a number last year, they have it. Do not contradict it without explaining what changed (more experience, higher current salary, market).

If asked in the room: give your number inside or at the top of the posted band, tie it to the range they published, and stop talking. "Based on the posted range and where I am now, I'm targeting the upper end, around $___." Don't negotiate in the interview. Negotiate when the offer arrives.

If you are also in process at Blank Metal or elsewhere and they ask, say so briefly and honestly, then bring it back to why you want this one.

---

## If someone remembers you

Warm, brief, forward: "Yes, good to see you again. I'm glad this worked out timing-wise." Then move on. Do not apologize for declining.

---

## Use Alex's slot to close

The 4:00 slot is described as answering your questions and hearing your impressions. It is also where the hiring manager decides how hard to push for you. Do three things:

1. **Say the intent out loud.** "I want to be direct: I'm very interested in this role and would accept an offer at the right number."
2. **Ask the timeline.** "What are the next steps and when do you expect to decide?"
3. **Ask the question from your personal list.** "Is there anything about my background you'd want me to expand on before we wrap?" It gives them a chance to raise a doubt while you can still answer it.

Give honest impressions when asked. Specific and positive about one thing from the day, plus one genuine question or observation. Empty praise reads as empty.

---

## Rehearse

Say the three-sentence answer out loud five times on Sunday. Once more Tuesday night. It should take under a minute and sound like a conversation, not a statement.
